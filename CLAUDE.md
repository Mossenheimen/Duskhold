# 12_Duskhold

An open-world fantasy survival RPG in Project Zomboid's style. The game comes first. Later it becomes a fast world for the PPO agent of `11_PPO-Zombie`, for training and maybe NPC AI.

- `CONCEPT.md`: what the game is. Decisions go in its "Decided" section.
- `ARCHITECTURE.md`: how it's built, the build order, and where the setup stands. Open questions are at the end; answered ones move into the text.
- `VISUALS.md`: how it looks: the camera, light, the colour tokens, shapes, surfaces, the drawn senses, type, the GUI's layout and parts, and how the AI and people work on the look. Read it before changing anything on screen, and change it in the same commit as the look.
- `crates/dusk-core/`: the simulation (no dependencies, no graphics, no files, no threads). `creature.rs` holds what a kind of creature is, and what it takes of a blow by weapon (`resist`: bone turns a blade) (a row of numbers in `Defs::creatures`; an actor of it is `ActorKind::Creature(n)`); `combat.rs` holds the one rule for timed actions and the fight (the swing, the heavy swing (`Heavy`): a long tell for more harm and a knock-down, the shove); `grab.rs` engagement, grabs, struggling and the drag-down; `items.rs` items, containers that fill from the seed, and the item actions; `body.rs` health, endurance and wounds; `needs.rs` the clock, hunger, thirst and fatigue, sleep, healing and moodles; `light.rs` day and night, each tile's light, and the torches and candles held up; `view.rs` the character's field of view and the fog of war; `skills.rs` skills that grow by real use under risk, repeats that pay less, the free cap and masteries, and what a level does; `barricade.rs` boarding up doors and windows, prying boards off, and the boards the dead beat through before the door; `home.rs` home (where you slept the night through), the stash, finds that pay their worth once, and character levels; `fever.rs` ghoul fever: caught from a wound, three stages a game day each, and the cure; `remains.rs` what becomes of a body: getting up again, burning it (oil and a flame), fire through a pile, ash; `injury.rs` wounds by where they land (worked out from the wounds: arms swing slower and less surely, legs walk slower and run dearer, the head aims worse and sees less far, the torso hurts more); `dread.rs` pain (worked out from the wounds: more breath, no sleep in agony) and dread (kept: rising toward what the dark, the dead near, pain and hunger are worth; shaky aim, and frightened, no step nearer the dead); `hunting.rs` beasts that flee (`Mind::Flee`) and wild hunters (wolves: `wild`, the dead's mind), neither counted as the dead, and butchering; `cooking.rs` cooking at a fire, and food that goes off by the hour (unopened containers aged with the world); `archery.rs` bows: an arrow along the line to the first one on it, walls stopping it; `shield.rs` shields on the off arm, bracing to block, the bash; `armour.rs` armour worn a piece to a place on the body: blows that glance off (no wound, a bruise), what it costs (weight, breath, noise, sight), the dead in armour and what gets through it; `climb.rs` climbing through an open window or over a fence (walking straight into it): a step in its own ticks, costing breath as running does, and the dead that climb by their kind, only after something, some tumbling in (`path::find_way` is the search that climbs); and windows' glass: beaten out by the dead that climb (or your blow), broken for good, cutting whoever climbs through; `spell.rs` spells: data (`spells.toml`), casting as a timed action (words that a blow breaks), a Wizard's slots back with a night's sleep, Arcana, and the four: Light (a lure at night), Silence (no sound made), Fog (the dead can't see in or out), Fireball (burns what it puts down); `stealth.rs` sneaking (a stance: slower, quieter, seen only from nearer), the sneak attack and Stealth; `bag.rs` pockets and the bag on your back: what fits (taking is bounded, nothing else is), what's in a bag weighing less and slower to reach, moving a stack across, and a bag dropped full (`World::held`) and taken up again; `cold.rs` wet and cold: the rain and the shallows soaking you, drying under a roof and by a fire, the cold drifting toward what the night, the wet and the rain are worth (less a roof, a bed, armour's warmth), shivering and freezing; `fitness.rs` Strength and Fitness: slow stats from 0 to 10, the day's breath spent and strain (blows, shoves, hauling) turning into growth at midnight, the more the less each, and what each does (carry, harm, a shove's knock-down; cheaper breath, quicker back); `craft.rs` crafting: recipes from what you carry with a tool to hand, and furniture broken down for planks; `fire.rs` a fire for your base: a hearth, stove or forge laid with fuel, lit from a light held up, burning down, put out, lighting its room, and drawing the dead that see it at night; `pressure.rs` the region's pressure (time and the risen raise it, burning lowers it) and the dead walking in where you aren't; `holdout.rs` the holdouts: hallowed ground (the dead never come in, nor heed you there), and the counter (buy from a daily stock, sell for coin, supply it, mend); `tolling.rs` the Tolling: the region's calendar, the bell, the dead drawn to it and not giving up till dawn; `flow.rs` flow fields, one Dijkstra map shared by a crowd going to one place; `class.rs` the classes (the Fighter and the Cleric), choosing one before you're under way, a class's kit, and talents: three offered a level, one picked, each changing numbers the rules already use, Cleave and Second Wind adding a little; `faith.rs` the Cleric's faith: prayer at a shrine, turning the dead (`Mind::Turned`: they flee, and a blow ends it), consecration (holy water or faith: `Remains::Hallowed`), Mending, wards on doors, Steady and Last Rites; `forage.rs` wild plants (`Defs::plants`), picking one (Y), plants growing back (`World::regrowing`) and Foraging, the remedies being items' own (a poultice is a bandage with a `salve`, a tea a drink with `comfort`); `weather.rs` rain: spells from the seed and the clock, lights put out, sound and scent carrying less outdoors, a darker sky; `magic.rs` magic items: rarity, properties and curses as data, unknown until read or learnt by use, a cursed weapon clinging; `rare.rs` rare monsters: affixes on top of a kind's numbers (`World::stats`), names, packs of finds, the Lantern-Eater; `legacy.rs` death and legacy: names, the body rising at midnight as a revenant in its gear, gear falling where a creature dies, the next character; `save.rs` (behind the `save` feature, the core's only dependencies) a world as bytes: the seed plus what changed.
- `crates/dusk-data/`: loads and checks `data/` (serde + toml), turning a designer's units into the core's.
- `crates/dusk-gen/`: a patch of a region from a seed: the layout (road, the market town's lots where there's room for the rest, the holdout, the river, the farms out in the fields), then every tile as a pure function, then the dead and the rares.
- `crates/dusk-cli/`: the demo, the benchmark, and the data and world tools.
- `crates/dusk-godot/`: the Godot extension (gdext). Its `DuskWorld` node makes a patch, ticks it, and hands GDScript the map a chunk at a time, the actors, what the dead can see, noises and events. It takes intents back.
- `data/`: the game's numbers and content in TOML: `rules.toml` (with `[faith]`, the Cleric's, `[foraging]` and `[cold]`), `creatures/`, `fixtures.toml` (an altar is a `shrine`; a wild plant has `forage`), `holdouts.toml` (the holdouts' trade, and which item is coin), `recipes.toml` (what can be made), `names.toml` (characters' and rares' names), `affixes.toml` (rares' affixes), `properties.toml` (magic items' properties and curses), `classes.toml` and `talents.toml` (the Fighter, the Cleric and the Wizard, their kits and talents), `spells.toml` (the Wizard's spells), `items/`, `loot/`, `prefabs/` (buildings as text grids; `town.toml` the shops, `farm.toml` the farmsteads, `graveyard.toml` the graveyard and its skeletons, `barrow.toml` the barrows and their wights, `abbey.toml` the holdout; the chapel, with its fonts of holy water, is in `hamlet.toml`), `regions/`.
- `client/`: the Godot 4.7 project, built in code. It draws what the core says and decides nothing:
  - `main.gd`: input, the camera and the loop; the window (the player's game maximised, a trial run 1600 × 900) and the GUI's size (`-`, `=`);
  - `look.gd`: the look's tokens, `VISUALS.md` in code: every colour, the text sizes, and the GUI's parts (`panel_style`, `text`, `bar`). Every script that draws preloads it, and uses a token, never a colour of its own;
  - `map_view.gd`: streams chunks round the camera, and draws the fog of war and the sight-cone overlay;
  - `chunk_view.gd`: one chunk's ground, trees, furniture and walls;
  - `actor_view.gd` (poses: the lean of a wind-up, lying down, dead), `tells.gd` (a swing's tell on the ground), `threats.gd` (engagement lines), `bag.gd` (the pack and "within reach" panels), `skills.gd` (the skills panel), `craft.gd` (the crafting panel), `spells.gd` (the spells panel), `zones.gd` (the spells lasting on their spots), `litter.gd` (what lies on the floor), `noise_rings.gd`, `hud.gd` (clock, body panel, moodle column, news, the sleep veil, death screen);
  - `shaders/`: the cut-away is `cut_away.gdshaderinc`, also used for tree crowns and furniture. `fog_of_war.gdshader` is a full-screen pass over everything opaque, before the overlays. Every surface is procedural (`surfaces.gdshaderinc`: noise, blocks, boards), worked out from where a point is: the ground by its kind (in the ground texture's alpha), and walls, furniture and trees by a surface code in each MultiMesh instance's custom data (`chunk_view.gd`'s `SURFACES`). Nothing is downloaded.
- `play.cmd`: builds the extension and starts the game.
- `research/`: what's behind the decisions.
  - `game_design.md`: the 2026-11-06 design research (combat, loot, athletics, levelling, pacing and more), with its sources. It was accepted and folded into CONCEPT.md the same day.
  - The pure-Python benchmarks of a tick and of its hot spots (any Python 3).

## Commands (PowerShell, from this folder)

```powershell
.\play.cmd                                 # build and play: carries on from the saved game (the first run also imports the project)
.\play.cmd -e                              # the same, but open the Godot editor on client/
.\play.cmd -- --seed=7 --walk=4,5          # a trial run, not saved. Flags after --: --seed, --town, --walk=X,Y, --zoom, --speed, --data=DIR, --class=NAME (a trial run asks for no class: the first, else), --do=S:ACTION (swing, swing_n/_e/_s/_w, heavy, shove, door, struggle, drop, take, dropbag, tobag, spells, cast_light, cast_silence, cast_fog, cast_fireball, sleep, drink, light, skills, save, board, unboard, burn, turn, pray, consecrate, ward, mend, pick, wear, keys, next), --news, --hour=H, --save=FILE, --shot=PNG --shot-after=S
cargo test --workspace                     # 373 tests, a few seconds
cargo run -p dusk-cli -- check             # load and check everything in data/
cargo run -p dusk-cli -- prefab cottage    # a building, in its four turns
cargo run --release -p dusk-cli -- gen --seed 7 --png map.png   # a patch: its lots, its dead by kind, the XP, cures and oil its containers hold, and a map (--text X,Y,W,H, --ticks N: also the pressure after, --tolling: its first Tolling, run)
cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets     # lints; keep it at zero warnings
cargo fmt --all                            # standard formatting
cargo run -p dusk-cli -- demo              # the scene, printed once a second of game time
cargo run --release -p dusk-cli -- bench --dead 100 --think-every 5   # ticks a second (ARCHITECTURE.md, performance budget); --tolling: with a bell drawing them
cargo run --release -p dusk-cli --example python_twin                # research/python_tick_bench.py, line for line in Rust
```

Godot 4.7.2 is in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Godot`. `Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe` is the editor; `Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe` prints to the terminal.

## Conventions

- **Build output** goes to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\cargo-target` (`CARGO_TARGET_DIR`), never into OneDrive. In the Bash tool, export it, and `PATH=$HOME/.cargo/bin:$PATH`, in each command: its shells don't see `setx` changes.
- **Determinism:**
  - no `HashMap` iteration in game logic (`hash::FastMap` is for lookups only: its hasher, `WordHasher`, is fast and nothing may depend on what it hashes to; `Fnv64` is for hashes that are kept or compared);
  - gameplay randomness only through `hash::roll(seed, tick, who, why)`;
  - actors in id order, and one world per thread.
- **Test maps are text grids** (`text.rs`), the same format as prefabs and `World::render`. Their `z` is creature 0, the test shambler (`Creature::default`).
- **A creature is data, never a rule.** A new kind is a `[[creature]]` in `data/creatures/`; if it needs a number no creature has, add the field to `Creature` (with its conversion and check in `dusk-data`), not to `Rules`. In a test, change a kind with `w.creature_mut(n)`, after any `w.defs = ...`: the numbers live in the definitions.
- **An affix is data too** (`data/affixes.toml`): what it does to a `Creature`'s numbers (`Affix::apply`). A rule asking what a creature can do asks `World::stats(id)` (its kind's numbers with its affixes on), not `World::creature(kind)`. Three hot paths read the kind in place for speed (the thinking beat, `sees`, `pace` for all but a rare): an affix that changed what they read would need them to ask `stats`.
- **The game's numbers live in `data/`,** in a designer's units (tiles a second, seconds, degrees). `dusk-data` converts them into the core's units and checks every reference: a new field needs its conversion and its check there. `Rules::default()` is a fixed set for tests and tools, not the game's tuning.
- **Saves:**
  - A new field on a saved type is saved by its derive, but it changes the format: raise `save::VERSION`. A new type inside a saved one needs `#[cfg_attr(feature = "save", derive(serde::Serialize, serde::Deserialize))]`.
  - World state added outside the actors and the grid (a new map in `World`, or the pressure) must be added to `save.rs`'s `State` by hand, and to `state_hash`. The save test's "same hash after loading" catches one that's hashed but not saved. What can be worked out from the actors (`World::fires`) isn't saved: `set_actors` works it out again.
  - The region (`World::region`) comes from the data, as the rules do: `dusk-gen`'s `patch` sets it, never a save. So do `World::hallowed` and the Tolling's bell; its field is worked out from the map (`Grid::edits` says when it's stale).
- **Buildings:** a new or changed prefab must leave every floor reachable from its door (furniture blocks corners: a diagonal needs both tiles beside it free). `dusk-gen`'s `every_floor_of_every_building_can_be_walked_to_from_its_door` checks it. Tests find buildings through the layout (`Layout::lots`), never by a seed's coordinates: the town moves when anything in it changes. A grid whose north side is open ground needs `+` corners on its first line: the parser drops blank first and last lines.
  - **Actors are only ever added,** never removed (bodies stay, as ash), so an actor's index in the bridge's arrays never changes. The client adds a view for each newcomer (`_add_new_actors`).
  - Rules and definitions are never saved: they come from `data/`.
  - **Any flag after `--` makes a trial run,** which neither loads nor saves the player's world (`user://world.dusk`). Test saving with `--save=<scratchpad>/x.dusk`.
- **Speed:**
  - a grid built by hand for anything that searches a lot (a tool, a benchmark) calls `Grid::index_steps()` once it's made, as `dusk-gen`'s `patch` does: searches then read a byte a tile. Unindexed, the answers are the same, slower;
  - before making something faster, time it (a tick's parts, as on 2026-11-10: `ARCHITECTURE.md`, performance budget), and keep the bench's figures honest: its character must stay alive (a dead one no longer keeps the world awake).
- **Generation stays order-free:** every tile and edge of a patch is a pure function of the seed, the layout and its place (`Layout::tile`, `north_edge`, `west_edge`), never of other chunks. `dusk-gen`'s tests check it.
- **Leave `11_PPO-Zombie` alone.** Don't edit it from here. Copy what's reused (`ppo.py`, telemetry, the dashboard) and note where it came from.
- **The client:**
  - **Rules live in the core.** A rule the client draws (sight, noise) comes from a core function (`World::sees`), never a GDScript copy of it.
  - **Generated files:** `client/dusk.gdextension` is written by `play.cmd` (it names this PC's build folder) and is gitignored, like `client/.godot/`. Commit the `.uid` files Godot makes next to scripts and shaders.
  - **Close the game before `cargo build`:** a running game holds the DLL. The editor loads a copy, so it reloads on a rebuild.
  - **Checking the look from the Bash tool:**
    - `cmd //c ".\\play.cmd --headless --quit-after 300 -- --news"` runs the scripts with no window, but it can't compile shaders. Keep a flag after `--`: without one it's the player's own game, and it loads and saves their world.
    - `cmd //c ".\\play.cmd -- --shot=<scratchpad>/x.png --shot-after=5"` opens the game for a few seconds on the user's desktop and saves a picture.
    - **Something timed** (the Tolling, a death): copy `data/` to the scratchpad, change the copy's numbers (`[clock] start`, a region's `[tolling] first` and `hour`, a need's `rise` and `harm`), and run with `--data=<copy>`. Headless runs about 300 frames a second, so `--quit-after` is in frames and `--do=S:` in seconds of play; a need's harm comes on the game hour.
  - **Colours:** client colours are picked in sRGB. MultiMesh instance colours go through `srgb_to_linear()`.
